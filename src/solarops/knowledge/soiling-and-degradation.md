# Soiling, degradation and module defects

## Soiling losses

Dust, pollen, bird droppings and agricultural or mining dust collect on module glass and block sunlight. In dry inland regions soiling losses can reach several percent within weeks without rain, while regular rainfall largely cleans panels naturally. Soiling shows as a slow, steady decline in the performance index across many clear days, followed by a jump after rain or cleaning.

## Deciding when to clean

Cleaning costs water, labour and access, so it should be justified by measured losses. Soiling measurement stations or a clean reference module compared against a soiled one give a direct estimate. The economics usually favour cleaning when the energy value lost before the next expected rain exceeds the cleaning cost. Cleaning methods and chemicals must follow the module manufacturer's guidance so that warranties and anti-reflective coatings are not damaged.

## Long-term degradation

Crystalline silicon modules lose output slowly over their life, typically around half a percent per year, with a larger drop in the first year from light-induced degradation. Degradation appears in the data as a very gradual year-on-year decline in weather-corrected performance, not as a sudden change. Comparing performance in similar weather across seasons separates degradation from soiling and seasonal effects.

## Potential-induced degradation, hot spots and cracks

Potential-induced degradation (PID) happens when high voltage between cells and the earthed frame drives leakage currents, reducing output of modules at one end of a string. Cracked cells, failed solder bonds and shading can create hot spots, and bypass diodes that fail short remove a third of a module's output. These defects are local, so they reduce performance by small amounts that are hard to see at farm level.

## Finding module defects

Drone or handheld infrared thermography finds hot spots, failed bypass diodes and disconnected strings quickly across a large site. I-V curve tracing of strings measures their current and voltage characteristics and confirms suspected defects. String-level current monitoring, where available, flags strings producing less than their neighbours under the same irradiance. Annual thermography is a common part of preventive maintenance.
