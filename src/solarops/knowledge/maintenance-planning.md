# Preventive maintenance and availability

## Preventive maintenance activities

A typical preventive maintenance plan for a solar farm covers inverter servicing such as filter cleaning, fan checks and firmware updates, tracker inspection and stow testing, module cleaning when justified by soiling losses, and annual infrared thermography. Vegetation must be managed so grass and weeds do not shade the lowest module rows or create a fire risk. High-voltage equipment in the substation needs periodic inspection, testing and protection checks.

## Scheduling around production

Work that needs equipment switched off should be planned for low-value periods, such as early morning, cloudy days or periods of expected negative prices. Planned outages of substation or connection assets are coordinated with the network service provider well in advance. Grouping tasks that need the same isolation reduces lost energy.

## Market notifications

A semi-scheduled farm must keep the availability it submits to AEMO accurate, including planned reductions from maintenance. Planned outages should appear in the farm's medium and short-term availability submissions so market forecasts reflect the reduced capacity. Inaccurate availability can cause dispatch and forecasting errors.

## Availability and performance reporting

Availability measures how much of the time, or how much of the capacity, was able to generate. Performance measures how well the available equipment converted sunlight into energy. Keeping these separate, and separating curtailed energy from both, avoids blaming equipment for market or network decisions. Clear loss categories make it easier to decide where maintenance effort pays back.

## Safety

All work on solar farms must follow site safety rules, permit-to-work systems and electrical isolation procedures. DC arrays remain live whenever there is light, even when inverters are off, so isolation and testing must follow the manufacturer's documentation and the site's switching procedures. This knowledge base is general guidance and does not replace site procedures or equipment manuals.
